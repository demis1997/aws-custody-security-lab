# Residual risks

The common cloud provider, organization administration, account recovery identities, human operators, shared artifact provenance, CA issuance and Terraform state can reconnect domains. An account boundary alone is not an independent cryptographic or administrative boundary.

Envelope keys do not hide live shares from an authorized compromised host. Key administrators can alter key policies. Two malicious signers may issue direct transaction authority. A compromised full authorization service may authenticate dishonest business intent within a permissive standing policy. State consistency under a malicious coordinator/database is not supplied by 2-of-3 signing.

Immutable backup versions do not supply a trusted current epoch. Joint state/anchor rollback passes the matching-pair test. Availability under real partitions, native protocol aborts and hardware/API incompatibilities remains untested. Monitoring detects selected actions, not every attack, and requires delivery, validation and human response.

The Terraform has one private subnet per workload, no installed software or high-availability data tier, no deployed SSM operator workflow, no automated application restore, no private RPC proxy, and no enforced two-person deployment/recovery approval. Those are concrete integration gaps, not implied protections.

Public-chain consensus, RPC finality, EOA quantum exposure, verifier correctness, post quantum signing, contract upgrades and incident asset migration require separate analysis. No compliance, penetration-test, performance, cost or production-readiness claim is made.
